## Maturity Level Descriptions

Level 1 (Initial):

SMA seeks to adopt enterprise-wide planning and architectural frameworks to improve program delivery. Current processes are unstructured, reactive, and inconsistent.

Level 2 (Developing):

SMA complies with federal regulations and guidance and has begun adopting MES industry-recognized planning and architectural frameworks. Basic processes and systems exist but are not fully standardized or documented. State-specific and MES metrics and performance data are collected and reported.

Level 3 (Defined):

SMA complies with federal regulations and guidance and has fully implemented MES industry-recognized planning and architectural frameworks. Processes, systems, and strategies are standardized, well-documented, and aligned across the organization. State-specific and MES metrics and performance data are actively monitored and analyzed for improvements.

Level 4 (Managed):

SMA maintains compliance, follows industry-recognized planning and architectural frameworks, and monitors MES performance to meet goals. Processes are fully operational, consistent, well executed, and State-specific and MES metrics and performance data are actively monitored and analyzed for improvements. The organization is a thought-leader in the MES ecosystem and is actively collaborating and sharing approaches with other SMAs.

Level 5 (Optimized):

The SMA employs advanced, data-driven strategies to manage MES planning and architecture, ensuring predictive decision-making aligned with its long-term goals. Integrated processes, technologies, and data drive enterprise optimization, while institutionalized innovation supports adaptability, scalability, and continuous improvement. The organization is nationally recognized and is actively collaborating and sharing solutions with other SMAs.

## Type and Data Description Mapping

| Type       | Maturity Level | Data Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Initial    | Level 1        | Processes that support the capability are ad hoc, informal, or inconsistent. Execution depends on individual effort and institutional knowledge, with limited documentation or repeatability. Outcomes are unpredictable.             |
| Developing | Level 2        | Processes are partially defined and repeatable for the capability, but application is inconsistent across teams. Documentation exists in some areas, and success still relies on key individuals rather than institutional practices. |
| Definition | Level 3        | Processes supporting the capability are formally defined, documented, and standardized. Roles, inputs, outputs, and handoffs are clear, and processes are consistently applied across the organization.                               |
| Managed    | Level 4        | Processes are measured, monitored, and actively managed to support the capability. Performance metrics, controls, and governance are in place, enabling predictable outcomes and effective management of risks and exceptions.        |
| Optimizing | Level 5        | Processes are continuously improved and optimized to maximize the capability’s value. Data, automation, and feedback loops are used to drive efficiency, effectiveness, innovation, and adaptability aligned to strategic objectives. |
