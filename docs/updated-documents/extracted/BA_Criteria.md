# Business Architecture Maturity Criteria

Extracted from: Business and Enterprise Architecture Maturity Criteria.xlsx

## 1. Business Process Performance

**Question:** To what extent is the performance of business processes supporting this capability consistently measured, monitored, and managed using meaningful metrics?

### Level 1

**Description:** Business processes that support the capability are identified and defined, but performance expectations are implicit and not formally measured. Process outcomes vary and are not consistently reviewed.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Capability mapped to supporting processes.
Process View: High‑level process definitions or catalog.
Artifacts: Process lists, capability maps, informal process descriptions.

### Level 2

**Description:** Business processes are documented in Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), including steps and accountable roles. Performance expectations are understood informally but are not yet measured or tracked.

**Evidence:** Process View: SOPs and Business Process Model with activities and role assignments.
Organization View: Role definitions linked to process steps.
Artifacts: Approved SOPs, role descriptions, training materials.

### Level 3

**Description:** Business processes are standardized and executed consistently across the organization. All staff follow the same documented process steps, and processes are centrally accessible through a Business Process Catalog.

**Evidence:** Artifacts: Approved SOPs, role matrix, training materials.
Process View: Standardized BPMN or workflow models.
Capability View: Capability Decomposition Diagram

### Level 4

**Description:** Business processes have defined performance metrics (e.g., timeliness, quality, efficiency) that are regularly tracked, monitored, and reported. Process performance insights are used to identify bottlenecks and manage outcomes. Select steps may be automated to improve performance predictability.

**Evidence:** Process View: End‑to‑end process models with KPIs.
Outcomes Based Planning Tool: Performance measures tied to outcomes.
Artifacts: KPI dashboards, performance reports, automation logs, leadership reviews.

### Level 5

**Description:** Business process performance is continuously assessed and optimized using metrics, trends, and feedback. Data‑driven insights are used to proactively improve effectiveness, efficiency, and capability outcomes through refinement, automation, and innovation.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Performance trends tied to capability health.
Process View: Continuous improvement models and backlogs.
Artifacts: Process improvement roadmaps, trend analyses, root cause analyses, optimization initiatives, Improvement Plans,
Process Assessments

## 2. Business Process Documentation

**Question:** To what extent are the business processes that support this capability identified, documented using defined standards, centrally managed, and regularly maintained?

### Level 1

**Description:** Business processes that support the capability are partially or inconsistently identified. Process documentation is incomplete, informal, or outdated, and business process models are not fully developed or consistently used to guide execution.

**Evidence:** • Partial lists of processes supporting the capability
• High‑level or informal process diagrams
• Draft or outdated BPMs with gaps
• Reliance on institutional knowledge to understand processes

### Level 2

**Description:** All business processes supporting the capability are fully identified and documented. Business Process Models are developed for each process and are available for staff reference, but modeling quality and consistency may vary.

**Evidence:** • Complete inventory of processes mapped to the capability. Process Catalog
• BPMs available for all supporting processes
• Process documentation used for onboarding or training
• Limited or informal documentation standards

### Level 3

**Description:** Business processes supporting the capability are formally defined and documented using a common modeling standard (e.g., BPMN 2.0 or organization‑defined conventions), ensuring clarity, consistency, and adherence to industry best practices.

**Evidence:** • BPMs compliant with BPMN 2.0 or internal standards
Centralized Process Catalog
• Modeling guidelines and documentation standards
• Verified consistency across process models
• Periodic BPM quality or compliance assessments

### Level 4

**Description:** Business process documentation is managed in a standardized modeling tool that supports version control, change history, and traceability. Process models are maintained in an enterprise business process catalog with defined relationships to capabilities and metrics.

**Evidence:** • Centralized modeling repository/tool
• Version history and change logs for BPMs
• Enterprise Business Process Catalog
• Documented relationships between processes, capabilities, and KPIs

### Level 5

**Description:** Business process documentation is managed in a standardized modeling tool that supports version control, change history, and traceability. Process models are maintained in an enterprise business process catalog with defined relationships to capabilities and metrics.

**Evidence:** • Formal review cadence for BPMs
• Evidence of updates driven by metrics or insights
• Process improvement logs or change requests
• Alignment of BPM updates with capability or strategy changes

## 3. Business Process Governance

**Question:** Are there individuals assigned to process governance roles that actively participate in the governance of the business process used to support this capability?

### Level 1

**Description:** Process governance roles and responsibilities have not been established or assigned to individuals for the business processes that support the capability.

### Level 2

**Description:** Process governance roles and responsibilities for the business processes supporting the capability have been identified and assigned to individuals (e.g., capability/business owner, process owner), and key process terms, definitions, and acronyms used to support the capability have been identified and documented.

**Evidence:** Process Artifacts: Process Catalog (with assignments,
Process Model View with Roles identified

### Level 3

**Description:** The SMA monitors the assignment of process governance roles to ensure minimal vacancy, and the assigned individuals actively participate in governing the business processes, including decision‑making related to process changes, standards, and alignment to the capability.

**Evidence:** Process View: Process Measures Catalog

### Level 4

**Description:** Process governance role assignments, process definitions, and key terms are stored in a central enterprise repository (e.g., business architecture or process catalog) and are accessible to stakeholders to search, discover, and understand the processes governed by the SMA.

**Evidence:** Central EA Repository

### Level 5

**Description:** Business processes adhere to enterprise process governance outcomes, policies, standards, and methodologies and are regularly assessed to identify opportunities to improve process governance, effectiveness, and alignment with the supported capability.

**Evidence:** Process Governance Diagram, SOPs, Process Governance Policy

## 4. Business Process Automation

**Question:** To what extent are business processes that support this capability deliberately designed, automated, governed, measured, and continuously improved?

### Level 1

**Description:** Business Process automation is minimal or nonexistent. Processes supporting the capability are largely manual, with heavy reliance on spreadsheets, email, and individual workarounds.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Capability defined at a high level with no automation attributes.
Process View: Informal process descriptions or institutional knowledge; no BPMN models.
Organization View: Roles implicit or unclear; automation ownership not defined.
Artifacts: Emails, spreadsheets, unstructured notes indicating manual execution.

### Level 2

**Description:** Isolated or tactical automation exists for parts of the process, often using basic tools or scripts. Automation efforts are reactive and focused on local efficiency rather than end‑to‑end capability support. Limited standards, reuse, or governance are applied.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Capability decomposition includes partial automation support notes.
Process View: Documented subprocesses with point automation identified.
Organization View: Local ownership for automation initiatives.
Artifacts: Tool‑specific workflows, scripts, or low‑code apps with limited documentation.

### Level 3

**Description:** Automation is formally designed and standardized for key processes supporting the capability. Common tools and platforms are adopted, processes are mapped prior to automation, and automation follows defined patterns. Documentation, roles, and controls are established.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Automation explicitly mapped to capabilities and outcomes.
Process View: Standardized BPMN models showing automated vs. manual steps.
Organization View: Defined roles for automation design, support, and ownership.
Information View: Inputs/outputs and data dependencies documented.
Artifacts: Approved process models, automation design standards, solution architecture diagrams.

### Level 4

**Description:** Automation is centrally governed, measured, and integrated across the capability. End‑to‑end processes are automated where appropriate, with performance metrics, monitoring, exception handling, and lifecycle management in place. Automation reliably supports business outcomes.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Capability heatmaps showing automation maturity and performance.
Process View: End‑to‑end automated process models with controls and metrics.
Organization View: Clear governance structures and RACI models.
Artifacts: KPI dashboards, governance charters, operational metrics, exception reports.

### Level 5

**Description:** Automation is continuously optimized and intelligent. Advanced technologies (e.g., RPA, orchestration, low‑code, AI/ML) are leveraged to improve speed, quality, and adaptability. Data‑driven insights and continuous feedback loops drive ongoing capability enhancement and innovation.

**Evidence:** Capability View: Continuous optimization tied to strategic capability outcomes.
Process View: Adaptive and event‑driven process models.
Information View: Analytics and feedback loops driving improvements.
Artifacts: Process mining insights, improvement backlogs, automation ROI analyses, AI‑enabled decision models.

## 5. Business Process Reporting

**Question:** Are the reports that are created to support this process documented and traceable to the specific data used to create each report?

### Level 1

**Description:** Reports and measures that are produced to support this process have not been identified and documented.

### Level 2

**Description:** Reports and measures that are produced to support this process have been identified and documented.

**Evidence:** - Report Catalog

- Measure Catalog
- Measure Specification

### Level 3

**Description:** Reports and measure standards have been established and implemented and are delivered to key stakeholders to support this process

**Evidence:** - Measure Standard

- Example Report
- Example Measure Calculation

### Level 4

**Description:** Detailed measure specifications and report definitions have been documented and are tracked to the impacted information used to produce the measure and report in a central metadata repository.

**Evidence:** - Metadata Repository Screenshots

- Metamodel
- Metadata Repository Operating Model
- Report/Measure Metadata Repository Change Workflow

### Level 5

**Description:** Reports and measures that are used to support this process adhere to enterprise business intelligence processes, procedures, standards, metadata and technology and are regularly assessed to improve the quality of the reports and measures.

**Evidence:** -Business Intelligence Process Diagram
-Business Intelligence Standard Operating Procedure
-Business Intelligence Standard Documentation
-Business Intelligence Metadata Requirements
-Business Intelligence MITA Scorecard
