# Enterprise Architecture Maturity Criteria

Extracted from: Business and Enterprise Architecture Maturity Criteria.xlsx

## 1. Business Capability

**Question:** To what extent are business capabilities defined, documented, governed, measured, and improved to support the enterprise?

### Level 1

**Description:** The capability has been identified and the use of the capability has been documented.

**Evidence:** Capability Catalog

### Level 2

**Description:** The SMA has begun the documentation of the capability roles, supporting processes, technology, and information. The capability complies with state and federal requirements.

**Evidence:** Role Catalog, Process Catalog, Technology Component Catalog, Information Asset Catalog

### Level 3

**Description:** The SMA has fully documented and defined the capability roles, supporting processes, technology and information. The capability complies with state and federal requirements and the conforms to MES industry - recognized architectural frameworks. The capability is standardized and aligned at the enterprise level.

**Evidence:** Performance Metricss, MITA SS-A, Central EA repository

### Level 4

**Description:** The SMA manages the capability for performance and compliance. The SMA monitors the metrics and measures of the capability.

**Evidence:** ConOPS

### Level 5

**Description:** The SMA uses performance monitoring to make improvements to the capability. The SMA is a thought leader for the capability and shares capability information with other SMAs.

**Evidence:** APD - Showing improvement or investment in the advancement or improvement of the capability.
Performance Metrics - SS-A, ConOPS

## 2. Enterprise Architecture

**Question:** To what extent is enterprise architecture integrated into MITA enterprise planning, governance, and execution to guide and optimize business, information, and technology outcomes?

### Level 1

**Description:** SMA recognizes the need for enterprise-wide planning and architectural frameworks but current EA practices are unstructured, reactive, and inconsistent.

### Level 2

**Description:** SMA complies with federal guidance and has begun adopting MES-recognized EA frameworks. EA processes exist but are not standardized or fully documented.

**Evidence:** Draft EA framework, initial architecture repository, compliance reports.

### Level 3

**Description:** SMA has fully implemented MES-recognized EA frameworks. EA processes are standardized, documented, and aligned across business, information and technology.

**Evidence:** EA governance charter, architecture standards, EA Repository

### Level 4

**Description:** SMA uses EA to monitor and manage MES performance. EA is integrated into strategic planning and execution. SMA collaborates with other SMAs to share EA practices.

**Evidence:** EA dashboards, strategic plans with EA inputs, collaboration artifacts. Outcomes Catalog, Business case diagram, EA Matrix

### Level 5

**Description:** SMA uses advanced, data-driven EA strategies for proactive decision-making. EA drives enterprise optimization and continuous improvement. SMA is nationally recognized for EA leadership.

**Evidence:** Predictive models, innovation roadmaps

## 3. Policy Management

**Question:** To what extent are policies systematically integrated into enterprise architecture, capability planning, and governance processes to guide compliance, decision‑making, and execution within MITA?

### Level 1

**Description:** Policies relevant to business capabilities are informally identified or inconsistently documented. There is little to no mapping of policies to business processes or systems.

### Level 2

**Description:** Some policies are documented and beginning to be mapped to business processes and systems. Compliance is mostly reactive, and regular reviews are not established.

**Evidence:** Policy Catalog, Policy/Process Matrix

### Level 3

**Description:** All business policies relevant to the capability (e.g., "Pharmacy Management") are identified, documented, and mapped to business processes and supporting systems. Policies are harmonized with state and federal requirements, and their alignment is regularly reviewed to ensure compliance and support for enterprise objectives.

**Evidence:** Policy/Process/Technology Component Matrix, Policy and Strategy Catalogs

### Level 4

**Description:** Policy alignment is institutionalized and integrated into enterprise governance. Policies are systematically reviewed, updated, and optimized based on performance data, regulatory changes, and strategic objectives.

**Evidence:** Performance Metricss, MITA SS-A, Central policy repository

### Level 5

**Description:** Policy alignment is a proactive, continuous process that drives organizational agility, compliance, and innovation. The organization demonstrates thought leadership and collaborates externally to share best practices.

**Evidence:** Outcomes based Planning Tool, Goal, Driver, Objective Catalog

## 4. Strategic Planning

**Question:** To what extent is the SMA’s strategic planning formally integrated into enterprise architecture, capability planning, and governance processes to guide the execution of MITA?

### Level 1

**Description:** The SMA’s strategic plan is informally considered during architecture and capability planning efforts. Alignment between strategy and capabilities occurs opportunistically and is largely dependent on individual initiative rather than a defined planning approach.

### Level 2

**Description:** The SMA’s strategic plan and capability planning are intentionally aligned through defined outcomes. Strategic objectives inform capability discussions, but dependencies between strategy, capabilities, and processes are not yet consistently analyzed or governed.

**Evidence:** Outcomes Catalog, Outcomes Based Planning Tool

### Level 3

**Description:** The SMA’s strategic plan explicitly identifies capabilities as required dependencies to achieve strategic outcomes. Capabilities and supporting processes are identified and analyzed as part of Enterprise Portfolio and Project Management to inform prioritization and planning decisions.

**Evidence:** Process Catalog, Capability Catalog, Enterprise Portfolio, Metrics Catalog

### Level 4

**Description:** The SMAs Enterprise Roadmap(Projects/Initiatives) have capabilities and processes identified that support the roadmap items. Capabilities and Process are identified and analyzed for intake of new business to support funding requests for an APD, or state funding for project approval

**Evidence:** Enteprise Roadmap, APDs, Intake Templates

### Level 5

**Description:** Capabilities and processes are core inputs to enterprise governance and decision‑making. Strategic planning, investment decisions, and roadmap prioritization are consistently driven by capability and process analysis to ensure alignment, maximize value, and support execution of SMA strategic objectives.

**Evidence:** Decision Logs, APDs
